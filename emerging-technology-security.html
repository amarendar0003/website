Cyber Security · Research Topic 05

Emerging Technology Security

Assess how new digital capabilities reshape the threat landscape and what safeguards support secure, responsible adoption.

Overview

Emerging technologies can expand institutional capability while introducing new dependencies, attack surfaces and governance questions. Artificial intelligence, cloud platforms and connected devices each have distinct security properties, yet they often interact through shared data, identity systems and suppliers. Security should be considered across design, procurement, deployment, operation and retirement.

Threat analysis needs to be specific to the system and its use. Broad labels such as “AI” or “cloud” do not by themselves describe an exposure; useful assessments examine architecture, data flows, trust boundaries, operational controls and likely consequences.

Research priorities

  • AI model, training-data and inference security, including misuse and adversarial inputs.
  • Cloud configuration, identity, shared responsibility and service resilience.
  • Connected-device and IoT asset discovery, update support and segmentation.
  • Deepfakes, synthetic media provenance, verification and response processes.
  • Emerging attack techniques, defensive testing and secure-by-design practices.

Threats and defensive strategies

AI-enabled systems may expose data, produce unsafe outputs or be manipulated through crafted inputs; safeguards include access limits, monitoring, testing and human review. Cloud incidents often involve identity, configuration or dependency failures, so teams need clear ownership and recovery plans. Connected devices can be difficult to patch or monitor and should be inventoried, segmented and supported through a lifecycle plan. Synthetic-media response should combine verification and trusted communication rather than relying on a single detector.

Governance considerations

Institutions should define intended use, accountable owners, data handling and supplier responsibilities before deployment. Privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility and operational requirements may intersect. Security testing must be authorised and proportionate to the environment. Where personal data or regulated services are involved, applicable legal and sector-specific duties need to be assessed for the particular system.

Future challenges

Rapid release cycles, opaque supply chains and evolving capabilities can outpace procurement and oversight processes. Long-lived devices may lack timely updates; models can change after retraining or integration; cloud and AI services may concentrate operational dependencies. Ongoing evaluation, clear contractual expectations, incident planning and periodic reassessment help institutions adapt as systems and threats evolve.

Research questions

  • How can institutions evaluate the security of AI-enabled systems throughout their lifecycle?
  • Which governance practices improve visibility into cloud and connected-device dependencies?
  • How should organisations communicate uncertainty when assessing synthetic media?
  • What procurement and assurance measures support secure adoption without locking in avoidable risks?

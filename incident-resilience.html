Cyber Security · Research Topic 03

Incident Resilience

Prepare to detect, coordinate through and recover from cybersecurity incidents while maintaining essential institutional services.

Overview

Incident resilience combines the capabilities to prepare for disruption, identify and assess events, coordinate an effective response and restore services safely. A written plan is only one element: people need clear authority, reliable contact routes, access to relevant information and practice working together under pressure.

Cyber incidents can affect confidentiality, integrity, availability, safety and public confidence at once. Response arrangements should therefore connect technical investigation with leadership decisions, legal and regulatory considerations, communications, business continuity and recovery.

Research focus areas

  • Incident response plans, roles, decision authority and escalation criteria.
  • Detection, triage, evidence preservation and internal coordination.
  • Reporting workflows and communication with relevant authorities and partners.
  • Business continuity, service prioritisation and disaster recovery planning.
  • Tabletop exercises, simulations, after-action review and improvement tracking.

Response and recovery challenges

Unclear ownership can delay containment and escalation. Teams may lack current asset maps, out-of-band communications or tested backups. External dependencies can limit recovery even when internal systems are restored. Organisations must also preserve relevant evidence, protect sensitive information, communicate accurately and make difficult choices about service restoration under uncertainty.

Exercises and continuous improvement

Tabletop exercises can test decisions, contact chains and coordination without disrupting production systems. Simulations can explore technical and operational scenarios where properly authorised and safely designed. After an exercise or real event, a structured review should identify strengths, gaps, owners and deadlines, then track whether improvements were completed and remain effective.

Institutional resilience outcomes

Clear response arrangements help institutions act more consistently, limit avoidable disruption and restore priority services in a controlled manner. Practised coordination can improve confidence among internal teams and external partners. Preparedness should be measured through tested capabilities and lessons applied, not merely the existence of a plan.

Research questions

  • How can institutions define escalation thresholds that support timely, proportionate decisions?
  • Which exercise designs reveal coordination gaps across technical and leadership teams?
  • How should continuity plans account for suppliers and shared-service dependencies?
  • What helps institutions translate incident lessons into measurable resilience improvements?
